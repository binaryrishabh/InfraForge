## Focused and generated verification

Focused tests cover analytical request/response serialization, propagation, setup
and CPU, persistent versus fresh connections, pool limits despite spare workers,
multiplexed leases, full dependency topology and canonical ordering, partition timeout
ownership, partial-byte bandwidth changes, propagation admission timing, health detection
and recovery lag, drain windows, retry idempotency, caller detachment, shared-connection
reset, idle expiry, Unknown/provenance refusal, endpoint suspension, buffer overflow,
zero bandwidth and distinct connect/acquisition/response outcomes. Returned evidence
mutation and changed sample cadence do not alter physics.

The **3,000-case deterministic network sweep** varies bytes/directions, bandwidth,
propagation, setup, connections, streams, pending bounds, worker/CPU capacity, arrival
rate, caller/acquisition/connect/response timeouts, routing, retries/backoff/budget,
drain, readiness and partition/delay/bandwidth/reset timing. Half use a multi-resource
LB/backend/dependency topology. Conservation is checked at an intermediate capture
and horizon; selected cases replay through different advance batches. These are
synthetic software-verification cases, not external transport validation.

Checks reconcile caller/compute populations, original/transmitted link bytes, pool
connection creation/closure/state and stream acquire/release/failure populations,
path outcomes/live exchanges, LB requests and nonnegative backend outstanding work.
Floating byte comparisons permit 1e-5 byte absolute numerical tolerance; byte inputs
and total counters are guarded for safe accounting and schedulable integer-us time.
No per-byte or packet simulation occurs.

## Reproducible mechanism proofs

[m2d-proofs.json](m2d-proofs.json) is generated by `bun run debug:network --write`.
All physics inputs are declared synthetic quantities with ledger provenance.

| Proof | Retained result |
| --- | --- |
| Connection reuse | Eight requests create one reusable connection versus eight fresh connections; later reusable requests avoid the authored 50-us setup through actual lifecycle. |
| Pool saturation | Forty callers with one connection/eight pending slots and spare compute workers produce two completions and 38 rejections/timeouts; workers cannot bypass the pool. |
| Multiplexing | Eight simultaneous operations share one eight-stream connection. This is logical multiplexing, not HTTP/2 performance. |
| Vertical topology | Forty roots traverse caller → LB → two compute backends → compute dependency and back, with actual health probes, readiness failure, a dependency response partition, recovery and backend drain. Thirty roots complete and ten fail at retained layers. |
| Health detection | A backend becoming not ready at 3,000 us is still observed healthy at 3,500 us; failed probe thresholds later change eligibility. After restoration, successful observations restore it. |
| Graceful/forced drain | Grace 5,000 us completes all 20 calls; zero grace completes 18 and rejects two. No new selection targets the draining backend. Existing remote work is not erased. |
| Retry amplification | Same 40 roots: no-retry has 40 LB attempts, 18,000 transmitted hop-bytes and 140,000 core-us; retry policy has 114 attempts (74 retries), 32,400 hop-bytes and 284,000 core-us. Three retries hit the concurrent budget. Neither case completes a caller; retries amplify actual work. |
| Dependency partition | Twenty-one of 25 roots complete; four fail. Owning timeouts and retained continuing work explain outcomes; healing does not retroactively resolve the old attempts. |

The storm proof runs long enough to retain all caller outcomes: 36 rejections/four
deadlines in each paired case, with late remote outcomes distinguished. Phase captures
show populations while failures/recovery unfold. Health probes remain separate roots
and consume real mechanism resources; they are not added to offered workload counts.

## Calibration tooling

The 90-batch campaign and 16 conditional establishment candidates replay exactly.
Tests check disjoint fitting IDs/configurations, application-byte and connection
counts, source hash, ledger validation and all proof artifacts. Corrupting held-out
throughput/response observations increases errors without changing candidates. The
complete host predictor is rejected because of large retained residuals; software
determinism/conservation does not establish calibration accuracy. See
[measurement and errors](m2d-calibration.md).

[m2d-performance.json](m2d-performance.json) records separate local Bun executor
timings. Four cases author 5,000 callers, actual request/response/dependency transfers,

These are local timings with uncontrolled GC/interference; they are not transport
capacity guarantees. Setup is about 7–26 ms, aggregation 5–10 ms, serialization
0.6–1.5 ms and serialized evidence about 178–568 KB. All workload roots are terminal.
At the horizon, health probes have legitimately started: two/eight/24/eight live
path exchanges and six/24/72/24 pending events respectively. They are explicitly
retained ongoing probe work, not declared drained or leaked roots. No acquisition
queue remains. Trace retains 100 events with complete type totals and about
221,754–241,748 omitted events labelled. Heap snapshots are GC dependent.

Links have one serializer boundary and propagation events per message/hop. Pool
connections, idle expiry, acquisitions and health checks have cancellable owned
timers. Completed connection objects and superseded callbacks are removed. FIFO
queue/connection scans are bounded and remain visible costs; measured cases did not
justify more complex indexes or a packet simulator. Event budgets fail explicitly
as incomplete evidence rather than silently dropping work.

Network policy limits are ledger-backed implementation guards: 512 links/paths,
16 hops/path, 512 connections/pool, 256 streams/connection, 100,000 unreservable pool
pending limit, 1,024 interventions, eight attempts, 100,000 authored probe starts and
1,024 identity characters. They do not describe physical hardware or provider limits.
Common workload/compute bounds remain applicable.
